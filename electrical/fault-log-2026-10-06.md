# Fault log — both drive-pack contactors open on the trailer (2026-10-06)

Field troubleshooting log. Author: Chris. Context:
[orion/README.md](orion/README.md) (how the two Orions are linked),
[batteries.md](batteries.md), [glorbmon/README.md](glorbmon/README.md).

## Situation

Glorb is on the trailer and needs to drive itself off into the garage. The
72 V drive battery is two Tesla packs, each on its own Orion BMS 2 and
contactor, behind a 1 / 2 / 1+2 / OFF selector switch.

- **One pack is bad.** Several cells are well out of line on voltage; some
  of its Tesla modules will probably need replacing.
- **The other pack is fine.** (Or so it looked — see "Outcome".)
- **Neither contactor will close**, so there is no drive power even though
  one pack is healthy.

## Root cause

The two Orions run Ewert's parallel-string mode (master + slave, talking over
the shared 250 kbit CAN bus via the `0x1850F3F3` message). When the bad
pack's unit faulted, the partner lost its link and raised **U0100 subcode
50** (lost master, parallel-string communication). That fault puts the unit
into Voltage Failsafe Mode: current limits ramp to zero and the relays drop.
So one pack's cell fault took both contactors down. Full write-up in
[orion/README.md](orion/README.md).

## Fix applied

Single-pack limp mode, per [orion/README.md](orion/README.md):

1. Selector switch on the **good pack only** (not 1+2), so the bad pack is
   physically out of the circuit regardless of what its contactor does.
2. Both units' profiles downloaded and saved to
   [orion/profiles/](orion/profiles/) before any change.
3. Good pack's Orion set to **Single Unit** on the Addon Settings tab,
   profile uploaded, unit power-cycled, codes cleared.
4. Contactor on the good pack closes; drive off the trailer.

## Freeze-frame analysis (P0A04 on the ex-slave unit, serial L59FA424)

Pack at rest (0 A), SOC 50 %, 115 of 230 Ah, cells 22–23 °C. Pack voltage
**66.3 V**, summed cell voltage **66.32 V**; the 18 cells below add to
66.33 V, so pack and sum agree.

| Tesla module | Orion cells | Voltages (V) | Sum | Spread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1–6 | 3.702 3.704 3.672 3.705 3.704 3.706 | 22.19 | 34 mV |
| 2 | 7–12 | 3.683 3.682 3.646 3.680 **3.910** **3.411** | 22.01 | 499 mV |
| 3 | 13–18 | 3.685 3.689 3.687 3.689 3.687 3.688 | 22.13 | 4 mV |

Cells 19–36 read 0.000 (unpopulated, as expected for an 18-cell pack on a
36-cell unit). Open-circuit cell voltages equal the instantaneous ones (pack
at rest).

What it says:

1. **One tap flagged: cell 11.** Highest cell = #11 at 3.910 V, lowest =
   #12 at 3.411 V. Nothing else is listed as open.
2. **Textbook open-tap signature.** Ewert's P0A04 document: "one cell
   voltage usually rises while the adjacent cell voltage drops". Here
   3.910 + 3.411 = 7.321 V, i.e. **3.660 V each**, within ~25 mV of the
   neighbours (3.68–3.69 V). The pack voltage matching the summed cells is
   exactly what a floating midpoint looks like: the pair's total is right,
   only the split between them is wrong. Two real bricks in series that
   happen to be 0.25 V high and 0.25 V low *and* sum to the normal value is
   not a plausible cell condition.
3. **Not a population-table boundary.** Tap 11 is the junction between
   cells 11 and 12, inside Orion tap group 1 (cells 1–12) and inside Tesla
   module 2 (its 5th/6th brick junction). It is not at the 12/13 group
   boundary or at tap 18, so the "unused taps tied to the top cell" rule for
   group 2 is not what tripped. Both saved profiles have all 18 population
   entries `true`, `totalCells` 18, and identical population tables.
4. **Module 2 is also the loosest module otherwise** (cells 7–10 span
   3.646–3.683 V). Ewert warns that a single open tap can skew other cells
   in the same group, so don't judge cell 9 until the tap is fixed.
5. **Knock-on limits.** DCL was already cut to 11 A (of 200 A max) "due to
   cell resistance" — the headroom calculation was using the bogus 3.411 V
   reading. Discharge-Enable output off. CCL still 100 A.
6. **No precharge in the Orion.** The profile has no precharge properties
   at all, and the frame shows *Precharge State 0*, *Application DC Bus
   Voltage 0*. Whatever precharge the drive has (if any) lives outside the
   BMS — in the contactor box or the motor controller.
7. Timing: Ewert tests each tap every 15 s and needs three hits on the same
   tap before setting the fault, so a fault at 135 s after power-on is
   consistent with a tap that was already bad (or marginal) at power-up.
   Whether P0A04 was logged on this unit *before* tonight is on the DTC
   tab's history; check it.

## Verdict (as of the first night; superseded by "Revised verdict" below)

**(b) Harness / tap connection on this pack**, specifically the tap between
cells 11 and 12 (the 5th/6th brick junction of the middle Tesla module, as
the Orion counts them). Reasoning: the high/low pair averages to the
neighbours, the pack sum is right, the flagged tap is mid-group, the
population table is unchanged and correct, and the car has just been
bounced down the road on a trailer. Ewert's own candidates in rough order:
loose or oxidised tap terminal, bad crimp, broken wire inside the insulation,
corrosion, or an internal BMS tap fuse (only if the problem stays on tap 11
when the harness is proven good).

(a) is ruled out by the data. (c) is not ruled out until a multimeter says
so, but it is the least likely reading of this pattern.

### Is a contactor-coil jumper reasonable for a 50 ft move?

Only with the gates the previous session set, and the data now sharpens
them:

1. **Multimeter all 18 groups first, selector on this pack only.** Measure
   at the module tap points *and* at the Orion connector for cells 10, 11,
   12 (Ewert: replace/inspect the flagged tap plus the one above and below).
   Expect ~3.66 V on each of 11 and 12. If either really reads 3.9 or 3.4 V,
   stop — that is a cell problem and this is not the good pack.
2. **Try the cheap fix before bypassing anything.** Disconnect the Orion's
   tap connector before touching the harness (Ewert's warning), reseat the
   tap connector(s) at module 2 and at the BMS, reconnect, clear the code,
   and wait at least 90 s (three 15 s tests). If the code stays clear and
   cells 11/12 read alike, the Orion will close the contactor itself and no
   jumper is needed. That is the preferred outcome.
3. **Precharge.** The Orion is not doing precharge, so normal operation
   already closes this contactor however the drive handles inrush. A
   coil jumper is therefore no worse than normal *in that respect*, but
   confirm where the precharge is (contactor box resistor? motor
   controller?) before relying on that, because a manual close onto a
   discharged DC link with no precharge stage is what welds contacts.
4. **If a jumper is still needed:** the Orion drives relays as low-side
   (ground-switching) outputs, so the jumper is the coil's return to
   ground, fused, through a switch you can open from the driver's seat.
   Keep the Orion powered and the utility connected on Live Text Data so
   cell voltages are watched during the move; creep speed; selector on this
   pack only; pull the jumper as soon as the car is parked. With the BMS
   bypassed there is no low-cell cutoff and no current limit.

## Outcome, continued: how it actually got off the trailer

Same night, after the analysis above. Sequence:

1. **Clearing P0A04 did not help.** On the ex-slave (pack 2) the code
   re-set immediately on every clear and the discharge relay never closed.
   Ewert's "three hits at 15 s" hysteresis does not buy time against a
   solidly open tap.
2. **No software override exists.** The profile has no open-wire or
   failsafe setting. Relay polarity inversion exists only for MPO1–4 and
   even those drop out on a critical fault; the Multi-Purpose Enable pin
   cannot be inverted at all (wiring manual p. 28). Discharge-relay options
   (require READY power, charge/ready interlock) all sit downstream of the
   Voltage Failsafe.
3. **Workaround that worked on pack 2: unpopulate cells 11 and 12.**
   Ewert's P0A04 document says an unpopulated cell listed under "open wire"
   does not set a code. With cells 11 and 12 unchecked in the cell population
   table and the profile uploaded, P0A04 cleared and the discharge relay
   closed. The unit is now blind to those two bricks (combined 7.32 V, i.e.
   healthy) and reports a 16-cell pack.
4. **What actually moved the car: the same trick on pack 1.** Chris
   unpopulated all the out-of-line cells on **BMS 1, the master (the bad
   pack)**, and drove Glorb off the trailer into the garage on that. Not yet
   recorded: which cells were unpopulated, which selector position was used
   (1 alone, or 1+2), and whether the master's multi-unit role was changed.
   Fill these in from the utility.
5. **Glorb is in the garage.** Later that evening both original profiles
   were re-uploaded, so the temporary cell-unpopulated configuration is
   gone; the units are linked again and faulted (below).

### Revised verdict: pack 1 is a wiring problem too

- **Master / pack 1 (top stack): three open taps, 3, 5 and 7** (the junctions 3|4, 5|6
  and 7|8). Module 1 and the first two bricks of module 2 are affected;
  module 3 is perfect. The modules themselves look healthy: module sums
  22.06 / 21.95 / 22.21 V are within 0.26 V of each other, and that gap is
  what you'd expect from modules that have not been balanced while the BMS
  was faulted. Cells 9 and 11 (3.59 / 3.62 V) may be slightly low for real
  or may be the open-tap distortion Ewert warns about; judge them after the
  taps are fixed.
- **Slave / pack 2 (bottom stack): one open tap, 11** (junction 11|12), unchanged.
- **All four bad taps are odd-numbered** (3, 5, 7, 11), and all are in the
  first 12-cell tap group. Four independent broken wires on two packs at the
  same moment is unlikely; look for a shared cause: a connector or harness
  row that carries the odd taps, a tap-fuse board, or something that got
  yanked during trailer transport.
- Nothing in the data says any Tesla module needs replacing. That cannot be
  confirmed until a meter has read the bricks directly (below), but the
  earlier "several cells well out of line, modules probably need replacing"
  conclusion was reading the open taps, not the cells.
- Other knock-ons visible in the snapshot: the master's SOC estimates
  disagree (94.5 % adaptive vs 50 % DOD-based, and 49.5 % on the slave at
  the same voltage) because its cell readings are garbage; cell resistances
  read a flat 0.76 mΩ on every cell of both units (never measured, the packs
  have been at rest).

### Multimeter plan (do this before anything else)

Selector **OFF**. Both Orions powered down on their 12 V side and their
cell-tap connectors **unplugged** before touching any tap wiring (Ewert's
warning: never adjust pack wiring with the taps connected). Then, for each
pack, measure at the **module** end first, then at the **Orion** end of the
harness:

1. **Brick voltages at the modules.** On each Tesla module, meter each of the
   six bricks at the module's own tap connector pins (adjacent pins =
   one brick). Expect every brick at 3.65 to 3.71 V on both packs. Write all
   36 numbers down in
   [tesla-batteries/battery_log.csv](tesla-batteries/battery_log.csv) or in
   this log. Any brick that really reads below 3.0 V or above 4.1 V is a
   module problem; anything in the 3.6s is fine.
2. **Same bricks at the Orion harness connector**, with the connector
   unplugged from the BMS: meter between adjacent tap pins (tap n-1 to tap
   n = cell n). On pack 1 do cells 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 first; on pack 2 cells
   11 and 12. A pair that reads 7.3 V across two pins with nothing sensible
   in between, or 0 V / open on one tap, locates the break in the harness.
3. **Continuity of the suspect taps**, harness unplugged at both ends: tap
   3, 5, 7 on pack 1 and tap 11 on pack 2, from the Orion-connector pin to
   the module-connector pin. Wiggle the wire at both crimps while watching
   the meter. Compare with a known-good even tap next to it.
4. **Look at what the odd taps have in common**: which row of the Orion
   connector they land on, whether they pass through a fuse block or an
   intermediate connector, and whether that part was disturbed on the
   trailer.
5. If the taps meter fine end to end and the fault stays on the same cell
   after reseating, the remaining suspect is the Orion's internal tap fuse
   (Ewert: >24 V between adjacent taps blows it; the unit then needs
   service). Test by swapping the two packs' harnesses onto the other BMS if
   the connectors allow it: if the fault follows the harness it is wiring.

After the fix: plug the taps back in, power up, clear codes, wait two
minutes, re-run `python -m glorbmon.obd2` and confirm every cell on both
packs reads within about 50 mV of its neighbours and no DTCs return. Only
then re-enable charging.

## Teardown (2026-10-08)

- Pulled the **middle module of the top stack** (pack 1). Top face looks
  fine; the **underside is heavily corroded**. Decision: pull all three top
  modules, then pull and inspect the bottom three as well.
- Why that fits the data: on a Tesla 6S module the series junctions between
  bricks alternate faces, so junctions 1|2, 3|4 and 5|6 sit on one face and
  2|3, 4|5 on the other. Every open tap we have (pack taps 3, 5, 7, 11 =
  module junctions 3|4, 5|6, 1|2, 5|6) is an odd junction, i.e. the same
  face on every affected module. If that face is the corroded one, the
  opens are the module's own sense leads / collector plates corroding, not
  the Orion harness. Confirm on the bench (below).
- Bench test per module as it comes out, before anything else: meter the
  module (expect ~22 V) and each brick at the tap connector (3.6–3.7 V),
  then put it on the Due with TeslaBMS and log it with
  `python3 tesla-batteries/battery_inventory.py log --id Bn`. TeslaBMS reads
  through the module's own BMB sense wires, so if it shows the same high/low
  pair the open is inside the module. Compare with the August rows in
  [tesla-batteries/battery_log.csv](tesla-batteries/battery_log.csv).
- Corrosion on the collector-plate face is also a safety issue (cell
  wire-bond fuses eat away, crust can bridge plates): do not energise or
  charge a module in that state, inspect for crust bridging plates, brown
  electrolyte stains, swollen or vented cells.

  control; the master's profile is the one carrying the Elcon frames.
